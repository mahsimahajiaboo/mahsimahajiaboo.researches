PROJECT 01Published · 2025

Chronotype and risky decision-making: an ERP study

My first-author MSc research examined whether morning- and evening-type participants differ in risky choices, response times and neural evaluation of feedback during a modified monetary gambling task.

FRN / P300Monetary gamblingChronotypeEEG/ERP
Read methods, findings & contributions
Study sample
218 individuals screened; final EEG sample of 39 (20 morning-type, 19 evening-type).
Experiment
384-trial modified monetary gambling task using MATLAB/Psychtoolbox and 64-channel EEG.
Analysis
EEGLAB/ERPLAB preprocessing and feedback-locked FRN/P300 analyses; mixed ANOVA and regression.
Published findings
Evening-types showed more risky choices, faster responses and attenuated FRN responses. No significant between-group P300 difference was found.
My contribution
Publication CRediT roles: Writing – original draft; Visualization; Investigation; Formal analysis.
